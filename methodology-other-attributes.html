Other Attributes

Attributes say how, for whom and on what an offering works: its audience, accountability, evaluate mode, simulation environment, object tested and benchmark.

They are capability flags, set independently of the lever scores.

Status: framework v3.1, 17 November 2026. The benchmark attribute has three flags since that day.

1Maturity

An attribute is set when a company has the capability at any maturity. Filters in the Company Assessment and the Market Map count shipping and early; claimed values are shown in the table, in the attribute list, with the maturity in brackets.

  • ShippingGenerally available and documented.
  • EarlyBeta, early access or preview.
  • ClaimedMarketing text only.

3The definitions in full

Each attribute states its values and where it applies.

3.1Audience

Who relies on what the offering produces.

  • InternalThe owner of the agent, including an agent configured on a vendor platform.
  • BuyerThe customer of an agent made by another company. It is set only when the company's own pages show a buyer or procurement use (R9).
  • Third PartyA regulator, insurer, lender, auditor, the owner's customers or the public.

Applies to Evaluate and Govern output. Control-only products are Internal.

3.2Accountability

Whether the party that issues a result carries a consequence if it is wrong.

  • AssuranceThe issuer attests and bears a consequence if wrong: an accredited certification or conformity body, a licensed professional attestation, or a certificate issued by a standard owner with its own technical testing.
  • InsuranceA carrier or an insured warranty transfers the loss.

Applies when the audience includes a Third Party. A vendor's own trust mark, badge or directory listing is not Assurance, whoever audits behind it; provisional or pilot accreditation is early; a warranty the company backs itself is claimed (R6).

3.3Evaluate mode

How an Evaluate offering gets its evidence.

  • Live trafficScores what the system did for real users, live or recorded, including per-call scoring.
  • SimulationThe product runs inputs it creates or holds: test sets, simulated users, red-team attacks, replays, synthetic calls to a deployed endpoint.

Applies to Evaluate 1 or more; both modes are allowed.

3.4Simulation environment

What a simulation runs against.

  • Prompt-levelInputs to a model or application, with no tools.
  • Mocked toolsThe agent calls simulated tools.
  • Stateful twinA copy of business systems that an agent can read and write: state persists, it can be seeded and reset, and the agent is graded on the end state. It is set only on product evidence, such as docs or a product page showing seeding, reset and persistent writes; a research benchmark or paper is claimed at most (R7).
  • Real system or sandboxThe agent runs against a real system or a sandbox of one.

Applies to Simulation.

3.5Object tested

What is being evaluated.

  • ModelA model, on its own.
  • ApplicationA conversational or LLM application.
  • Agent with toolsAn agent that calls tools and acts.

Applies to Evaluate 1 or more; all values are allowed in both modes.

3.6Benchmark

Three independent flags, each with its own maturity.

  • Custom judgeThe product's main, marketed function is to judge an AI system against the customer's own written policy, rules or expert criteria and return scored results with reasons. A generic ability to write your own metric or judge does not count.
  • Generalized benchmarkThe company itself maintains and markets a named test set, benchmark or standard that is versioned or dated and has defined scoring or pass criteria. It includes sets and standards tied to a regulation, for example a named EU AI Act test suite or a certification standard with defined controls. It does not include templates merely aligned to a regulation, metric libraries, framework mappings, third-party benchmarks the company only reports on, or research datasets it does not market as a benchmark.
  • Public leaderboardScores published openly for comparison across vendors or models. It always requires a generalized benchmark, and a private buyer leaderboard does not count.

Applies to any company. Regulation and standard benchmarks are folded into the generalized benchmark (R8).

5Descriptive columns

The list also shows Stage, Scope, headquarters, year founded and funding. They are descriptive, not scored, and have no fixed definitions: each company's cell gives the reason for its label in a sentence.
