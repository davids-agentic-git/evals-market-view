Two governing frameworks

The AI Control Value Levers describe what a product is sold for. The Evaluation Framework describes what it checks. Every company in the view is scored on both.

AI Control Value Levers

The AI Control Value Levers describe what an AI evaluation and assurance product is sold for. There are five: Govern, Test, Observe and prove, Control and enforce, and Assure and insure. Each company is scored from 0 to 3 on every lever, by its focus: what the offering mainly consists of, not everything it offers.

A score of 2 or 3 counts as high on a lever; 0 and 1 do not. The levers say where in the lifecycle a product acts. The four dimensions of the Evaluation Framework say what it checks, and every company is scored on both.

1The five value levers at a glance

Each lever is defined by what it does with an AI system and where it acts. Section 2 gives every lever in full, and section 3 explains how a company is scored.

Value leverIn one lineWhere it acts
Govern Keeps a register of a company's AI systems and agents and sets what each may do. On records: it decides and records, and does not enforce at run time
Test Probes an AI system with inputs the product creates or holds, separate from real user traffic, and scores the responses against criteria. Before release, in a build pipeline or on a schedule, on its own inputs
Observe and prove Turns what live systems actually did for real users into measurements, scores, reports and records. On live activity, after the fact: it describes the past and does not change the outcome
Control and enforce Sits in the live path and changes what happens before an action completes. In the live path, before the action completes: it acts, and does not only report
Assure and insure Gives people outside the AI's owner a result about the AI system that they can rely on, or moves the loss to a third party. Outside the AI's owner: a result issued by another party, or a loss carried by one

2The definitions in full

Each lever states what it covers, what it does not, and what its scores mean. The scoring rules that apply to all five levers are in section 3.

2.1Govern

Whether a product keeps a register of a company's AI systems and agents and sets what each may do.

Covers
Discovery or a register tied to rules, risk and regulation assessments recorded per system, approvals before a system or action goes ahead, and entitlement records for agent identity. It decides and records; it does not enforce at run time.
Does not cover
Discovery alone with no rules. A generic compliance checklist not tied to specific AI systems. Enforcing the rules at run time (Control and enforce).

Score

  • Score 3A main part and at least two of these cues: automated discovery that feeds the register; risk assessment mapped to regulations; policies and approvals tied to specific systems; re-assessment when a system changes; review of agent entitlements.
  • Score 2A main part of the offering that meets this description on the product's main paths.
  • Score 1Offered but not a main part. Discovery alone scores 1.

2.2Test

Whether a product probes an AI system with inputs it creates or holds, apart from real user traffic, and scores the responses.

Covers
Evaluation suites, red teaming, agent simulations, bias and regulation test packs, and evidence packs produced from test runs. Responses are scored against criteria for quality, compliance or security. It can run before release, in a build pipeline, on a schedule against the live system, or by outside testers working for the owner.
Does not cover
Static scans of code, models, prompts or policies. A one-off scan with no scoring. Scoring of real production traffic (Observe and prove).

Score

  • Score 3A main part and at least two of these cues: repeatable suites with datasets and version comparison; adversarial testing or agent simulation; domain or regulation test packs; tests that run on every change or continuously and can stop a release.
  • Score 2A main part of the offering that meets this description on the product's main paths.
  • Score 1Offered but not a main part. Static scans score 1.

2.3Observe and prove

Whether a product turns what live systems did for real users into measurements, scores, reports and records.

Covers
Production traces with scoring, quality, cost and drift dashboards and alerts, and reports mapped to a regulation. At the top end it adds signed or tamper-evident records and an incident workflow. It describes the past and does not change the outcome.
Does not cover
Storing logs only. The product's own enforcement decision logs and block counts. Results of test runs (Test). Alerts that block (Control and enforce).

Score

  • Score 3A main part and at least two of these cues: scoring of production traffic for quality and compliance; drift and cost scoring; reports mapped to a regulation; signed or tamper-evident records; an incident workflow.
  • Score 2A main part of the offering that meets this description on the product's main paths.
  • Score 1Offered but not a main part. Storing logs only, or the product's own enforcement logs, scores 1.

2.4Control and enforce

Whether a product sits in the live path and changes what happens before an action completes.

Covers
Gateways, proxies, SDKs, sidecars and approval steps that block, redact, rewrite, reroute or hold a call or action through a stated mechanism. It includes identity-aware checks (authenticating the agent, per-call permission checks, credential brokering) and spend caps that stop calls when a budget is used up. It acts; it does not only report.
Does not cover
Alerts, dashboards and after-the-fact termination. Warn-only modes. Defining the rules (Govern). A claim of real-time blocking with no stated mechanism.

Score

  • Score 3A main part and at least two of these cues: enforcement across the main paths (prompts, tool calls, data, agent-to-agent); decisions that use agent identity, permissions, task history or a baseline; approval steps; a kill switch; a fail-closed option.
  • Score 2A main part of the offering that meets this description on the product's main paths.
  • Score 1Offered but not a main part. Alerts and warn-only modes score 1.

2.5Assure and insure

Whether a product gives people outside the AI's owner a result about the AI system that they can rely on, or moves the loss to a third party.

Covers
Two forms. Attestation: a score, report, audit opinion, certificate or trust mark issued by a party other than the one running the AI, including the testing behind another body's certificate and evaluations that a frontier lab publishes with the evaluator named. Risk transfer: insurance, an insured warranty or a guarantee, and brokered placement.
Does not cover
Reports that only the customer's own team reads. Benchmark scores nobody commissioned. The company's own certifications, such as its own SOC 2 or ISO. The evaluation or test itself (Test): this lever is the vouching on top of it.

Score

  • Score 3A main part and at least two of these cues: issued under accreditation or regulatory designation; a published standard or scheme with independent assessors; named parties that rely on the result (insurers, regulators, agencies); a public directory or registry of results; surveillance or re-assessment when the system changes; a bound policy or insured warranty; a named carrier or reinsurer that bears the loss.
  • Score 2A main part of the offering that meets this description on the product's main paths.
  • Score 1Offered but not a main part: evidence made for outsiders but issued by the company's own tool, or an offer with nothing bound or no independent assessor behind it.

3How a company is scored

Every company is scored on the depth scale below for each of the five levers. The scores follow the focus of the offering: what it mainly consists of, not everything it offers.

The depth scale

  • Score 3Main part and leading. A main part of the offering and at least two of the lever's cues, listed with each lever in section 2.
  • Score 2Main part. A main part of the offering that meets the lever's description on the product's main paths.
  • Score 1Supporting. Offered but not a main part: a feature, a beta or announced offer, logs or static scans only.
  • Score 0None. Not offered.

A score of 2 or 3 counts as high on a lever; 0 and 1 do not.

The scoring rules

  • Prominence decidesA function that is a main part of the offering scores 2 or 3. One offered as a secondary feature or an add-on scores 1. Mentioning a function, or a long feature list, does not make it a main part.
  • EvolutionTest, Observe and prove, and Control and enforce are treated as an evolution. A company usually has one home among them and scores 1 on the earlier functions it offers as supporting features.
  • Two highsA company is high on two of the three only when the two have equal prominence: testing and production scoring as the two halves of one product, or two named pillars such as red teaming and runtime guardrails. A suite that strings functions in a pipeline is not a tie.
  • Independent leversGovern and Assure and insure are scored independently of the other three.
  • Lower score when in doubtWhere two readers could disagree on how prominent a function is, the lower score is used.
  • A mechanism is requiredEvery 2 or 3 needs a stated mechanism. A claim such as "real-time protection" without one scores at most 1.
  • Beta and announced offersA capability in private beta, preview, early access or "coming soon" scores 1.
  • One feature, one leverDefining rules is Govern, checking them at run time is Control and enforce, and logging the check is at most Observe and prove 1.

Evaluation Framework

The Evaluation Framework separates what a product evaluates into four dimensions: Output quality, Cost and performance, Security, and Behavior and compliance. Each company is rated yes, partly or no on each dimension, based on what its published pages show.

It describes what is evaluated, not when: the same dimension can be checked before release, on production traffic or in the live path. Section 3 sets out the test that decides which dimension a failure belongs to.

1The four dimensions at a glance

Each dimension is defined by what it evaluates and the kind of failure it looks for. Section 3 explains how a failure is assigned to a dimension.

DimensionIn one lineThe failure it looks for
Output quality Correct and useful outputs in ordinary use, measured as its own test type (accuracy, groundedness, task success). An output that is wrong or not useful, in ordinary use
Cost and performance Spend, tokens, latency and routing. Not covered by the context test
Security Attacks and abuse and the data exposure they cause: prompt injection, jailbreaks, leakage, tool and permission abuse, supply chain, rogue agents. A failure that occurs only when someone attacks or misuses the system, or when sensitive data leaves it
Behavior and compliance Conduct (outputs, decisions, behaviour) judged against a regulation, standard, fairness requirement or the customer's own content or conduct policy, with conformance reported. Security tests mapped to frameworks and attack-related content filters do not count. Conduct that breaks a rule, in ordinary use

2The definitions in full

Each dimension states what it covers, what it does not, and how it is rated on one yes / partly / no scale. Dimension-specific rating rules exist only for Security and for the "separate named module" requirement on Output quality and Behavior and compliance.

2.1Output quality

Whether a product checks that outputs are correct and useful in ordinary use, as a test type of its own.

Covers
Whether outputs, answers or actions are correct and useful in ordinary use, measured as its own test type with scores or metrics: accuracy, groundedness and faithfulness, hallucination, relevance, consistency, task success, and performance and stability tests in model validation.
Does not cover
Attacks and leakage (Security), conduct against a rule (Behavior and compliance), spend and latency (Cost and performance). Hallucination, off-topic or unsafe-output probes that are items in an attack catalogue or in a guardrail's detector list.

Rating

  • YesA main part of the product evaluates correctness or usefulness as its own test type, and the pages name the criteria, checks, metrics or test types.
  • PartlyA supporting or limited check, a claim without named criteria, or only part of the product (a beta, one surface). It needs a separate named module or test type for output quality.
  • NoNot evaluated, no evidence on the pages, or only one item in a risk list, taxonomy or detector list of a security product.

2.2Cost and performance

Whether a product measures what it costs to run: spend, tokens, latency, and the choice of model or prompt.

Covers
Spend, tokens, latency, budgets, model or prompt choice and routing.
Does not cover
Quality of the outputs themselves.

Rating

  • YesA main part of the product evaluates spend, tokens, latency or routing, and the pages name the criteria, checks or metrics.
  • PartlyA supporting or limited check, a claim without named criteria, or only part of the product (a beta, one surface).
  • NoNot evaluated, or no evidence on the pages.

No cost-specific rule is written; this is the general scale.

2.3Security

Whether a product tests or enforces against attacks and abuse of AI systems, and the data exposure they cause.

Covers
Attacks and abuse of AI systems and the data exposure they cause: prompt injection, jailbreaks, data leakage and PII exposure, tool and permission abuse, supply-chain compromise of models and agent tools, rogue or anomalous agent behaviour, and harmful or toxic output filtered as protection against misuse. Mapping security findings to a framework, standard or regulation is security reporting.
Does not cover
Governance records such as inventory, owners and entitlements (Govern). The correctness of ordinary outputs (Output quality) and conduct against a rule in ordinary use (Behavior and compliance).

Rating

  • YesA main part of the product tests or enforces against named attack classes, a catalogue or a framework.
  • PartlySecurity is a supporting feature, is claimed without a stated mechanism or catalogue, or is limited to inventory or posture (AI-SPM, AI-BOM) without testing or enforcement.
  • NoNot evaluated, or no evidence on the pages.

2.4Behavior and compliance

Whether a product judges the system's conduct against a rule and reports whether it conforms: a regulation, a standard, a fairness requirement or the customer's own policy.

Covers
Whether the conduct of the system, meaning its outputs, decisions or behaviour, meets a rule, and the product reports conformance as a named function: a regulation (for example the EU AI Act, ECOA/Reg B, SR 11-7), a standard, a fairness requirement, or the customer's own content or conduct policy (allowed topics, tone, required disclosures, brand rules, harm categories tested in ordinary use). For governance products it also covers assessing AI systems against a regulation as the main evaluation.
Does not cover
Content moderation (toxic, harmful, unsafe) and PII, PCI or PHI leak detection inside a guardrail against attack, misuse or data loss. Any security test or guardrail whose findings are mapped or reported against a framework, standard or regulation, such as OWASP, MITRE, NIST, ISO, the EU AI Act, DORA or HIPAA: that is security reporting. Correctness of answers (Output quality).

Rating

  • YesA main part of the product judges conduct against a rule and reports conformance as a named function, and the pages name the criteria, standards or test types.
  • PartlyA supporting or limited check, a claim without named criteria, or only part of the product (a beta, one surface). It needs a separate named module or test type for conduct and compliance.
  • NoNot evaluated, no evidence on the pages, or only one item in a risk list, taxonomy or detector list of a security product. A governance register that only records is not an evaluation of any dimension.

3The context test

Some failures could belong to more than one dimension, harmful or toxic content for example. The dimensions are told apart by the situation in which the failure occurs, not by the topic.

  • A failure that occurs only when someone attacks or misuses the system, or when sensitive data leaves itSecurity
  • A failure in ordinary use where the output is wrong or not usefulOutput quality
  • A failure in ordinary use where the conduct breaks a ruleBehavior and compliance

Harmful or toxic content is judged by this test. Produced by an attack, or filtered by a guardrail whose purpose is to stop misuse, it is Security. Measured against a defined content or conduct policy in ordinary use, as its own named test pack or evaluator, it is Behavior and compliance.

The dimensions are independent of the AI Control Value Levers (Govern, Test, Observe and prove, Control and enforce, Assure and insure): the same dimension can be checked before release (Test), on production traffic (Observe and prove) or in the live path (Control and enforce).
